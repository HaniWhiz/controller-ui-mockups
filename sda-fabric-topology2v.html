SD-Access Fabric Topology — Interfaces, Throughput & TCAM Resources

One fabric site for about 2,000 users, built from 2 × Catalyst C9500-24Y4C nodes that combine the control plane, an anywhere border and the underlay core, 4 × Catalyst C9400 intermediate nodes in two groups, and 60 × Catalyst C9300 edge nodes. Every physical link with its exact interface, transceiver type, speed and aggregate capacity, plus the SDM/TCAM and SD-Access scale limits that apply, with Cisco sources.

Design decision: the control plane runs on the two C9500-24Y4C borders instead of on dedicated C9500-12Q nodes. About 8,000 endpoints is roughly 5% of the C9500-24Y4C's CP scale, while the C9500-12Q is end-of-sale and its IOS XE support ends with 17.18.[11][15] Compare the options

Throughput & oversubscription

Control Plane nodes · CP/BN-1 / CP/BN-2 · C9500-24Y4C

Control-plane resources: what actually limits a CP node

Cisco publishes no single "Control Plane TCAM" figure. Cisco sizes an SD-Access control-plane node by its host tracking database (HTDB) of LISP EID-to-RLOC bindings, its CPU and its memory.[12] Catalyst Center rates CP capacity in endpoints: 150,000 for the Catalyst 9500 High Performance, listed as “9500H”.[11] The Map-Server/Map-Resolver database lives in IOS XE software memory, not in TCAM. Hardware TCAM and FIB resources matter for traffic the node forwards, which here means the co-located border and the underlay core.
AHardware forwarding TCAM / tables

UADP 3.0 forwarding tables

These live in the ASIC and are partitioned by the SDM template.

  • FIB: 416,000 double-width entries shared by IPv4 and IPv6, at the same scale for both[2]
  • ACL TCAM: 54,000 entries in total[2]
  • Templates: Distribution, Core (default), NAT, SD-Access. Custom SDM from IOS XE 17.3.1.[1]
  • IP/mask programming is hash-based, so usable capacity depends on the prefix mix[1]
  • Used for border VXLAN encap/decap, LISP map-cache entries merged into CEF, and underlay forwarding[12]
BRouting / host resources

LPM, host, adjacency and MAC

These are allocations within the FIB as published per SDM template.

  • IPv4/IPv6 LPM + host: 212K (SD-Access template). Up to 256K routes with custom SDM.[1]
  • Host/ARP: 80,000 adjacencies on SVIs (Distribution template), or 90,000 direct routes on routed ports (all templates)[1]
  • MAC: 32K (SD-Access) · 82K (Distribution) · multicast routes 32K[1]
  • Border scale: 48,000 IPv4 routes · 150,000 fabric host entries (/32 or /128, with every address counted)[11]
CACL / security resources

Security ACL, QoS ACL, SGT

These are TCAM-backed classification entries.

  • Security ACL 27K · QoS ACL 16K (SD-Access template)[1]
  • MPLS/SGT labels: 32K[1]
  • Border scale: 200,000 IPv4–SGT bindings · 16,000 SGT/DGT policies · 13,000 SG-ACEs[11]
  • Group-based policy (SGACL) uses security ACL TCAM wherever it's enforced, which is normally the egress edge
DSoftware / control-plane resources

IOS XE on x86: where LISP lives

These scale with CPU and memory, not with TCAM.

  • x86 2.4 GHz 4-core CPU · 16 GB DDR4 · 16 GB flash[1][2]
  • LISP Map-Server/Map-Resolver (HTDB), LISP Pub/Sub or LISP/BGP, IS-IS, BGP to the fusion, and CTS all run as IOS XE processes
  • CP scale: 150,000 endpoints, against about 6,000–8,000 for this site[11]
  • Two CPs of the same model run active/active, and borders and edges register with both.[12]
  • With frequent wireless roaming, a co-located CP/BN can hit high CPU, and the CVD recommends dedicated CPs in that case.[12]

Hardware & design notes

01Why C9500-24Y4C as co-located CP + anywhere border

  • About 2,000 users means roughly 6,000–8,000 endpoints. Catalyst Center rates the Catalyst 9500 High Performance at 150,000 CP endpoints, 150,000 border host entries and 48,000 IPv4 routes, so the pair runs at about 5% of its CP scale.[11]
  • Cisco says a co-located CP is the most common deployment, and recommends dedicated CPs mainly where frequent wireless roaming drives CP CPU load. Neither applies to a 2,000-user headquarters.[12]
  • Two switches do three jobs: CP, anywhere border (internal + external, handing every VN to FUS-1 and FUS-2) and underlay core. That's two fewer switches, four fewer links and two fewer licences than dedicated CPs.
  • The 4 × 40/100G QSFP28 ports give exactly one 100G downlink to each intermediate. The 24 SFP28 ports cover the cross-link (1–2) and the handoffs (23–24), with 20 left for a WLC, extra handoffs or dedicated CPs later. All four QSFP28 ports are used, so more intermediates would need 25G downlinks or a C9500-48Y4C / C9500X-28C8D.[1][3]
  • It has the same UADP 3.0 tables and SDM templates as the C9500-48Y4C. Its 2.0 Tbps and 1 Bpps are well above the 500 Gbps of ports in use.[1][2]

02Why not dedicated C9500-12Q control-plane nodes

  • The C9500-12Q is end-of-sale (last order 30 April 2024). Software maintenance ended on 30 April 2025, IOS XE 17.18 is its last release, and support ends on 30 April 2029. Cisco's replacement is the C9500-32QC.[15]
  • Its 12 ports are 40G QSFP+ only, so it can't run 25G. Links from the borders' SFP28 ports would run at 10G at most.[14]
  • New SD-Access features and fixes after 17.18 wouldn't reach the CPs that every edge and border depends on. The pair would also add two switches and four links without adding capacity this site needs.
  • If dedicated CPs are ever needed, use two of the same current model, for example another C9500-24Y4C pair on two spare SFP28 ports per border.[12]

03Why C9400 (C9404R + SUP-2XL + LC-48XS) as Intermediate

  • Intermediate nodes only route the underlay. They need IP reachability and MTU, not LISP, VXLAN or SGT support, so the choice comes down to port density and bandwidth.[12]
  • The C9400-LC-48XS provides 48 × 1/10G SFP+ fiber ports: 30 for edge nodes and 18 spare. With SUP-2XL each slot gets 480 Gbps (1:1). With SUP-2 it would get 240 Gbps (2:1).[6]
  • The SUP-2XL provides the QSFP28 100G uplinks, 3 Bpps of forwarding and a 108 MB buffer. Its 4 × SFP28 ports stay spare: there are no intermediate cross-links, because each intermediate already has two 100G paths to the CP/BN pair and every edge is dual-homed.[5]
  • The C9404R is the smallest chassis. Slots 1 and 4 take line cards and slots 2 and 3 take supervisors, which leaves room for a growth line card and a redundant SUP-2XL.[4][7]
  • On the SUP-2XL, enabling port 5 disables ports 1–4. This design uses Hu2/0/6–7 and leaves port 5 unused.[7]

04Why C9300 (C9300-48U + NM-8X) as Edge

  • Catalyst Center edge scale for the 9300 is 6,000 endpoints, 256 VNs, 8,000 SGT/DGT policies, 5,000 SG-ACEs, and 200 APs with 4,000 wireless endpoints. That's ample for a 48-port access switch.[11]
  • 48 × Cisco UPOE (60 W) copper ports serve APs, phones and PCs. The NM-8X adds 8 × 1/10G SFP+ ports: 2 used as uplinks and 6 spare.[9]
  • UADP 2.0, 256 Gbps switching and 190.48 Mpps, with an x86 CPU and 8 GB DRAM.[9]
  • The switches are standalone here. A StackWise-480 stack added later counts as one fabric device.[9][11]

05Copper vs fiber interfaces

  • Copper (RJ-45): Gi1/0/1–48 on every C9300-48U (2,880 ports, 10/100/1000BASE-T, UPOE), and Gi0/0 out-of-band management on every device.
  • Fiber (pluggable optics): every switch-to-switch link. That's QSFP28 100G for CP/BN ↔ intermediate, SFP28 25G for CP/BN ↔ CP/BN and border ↔ fusion, and SFP+ 10G for intermediate ↔ edge.
  • Choose SR multimode or LR single-mode optics by distance and cable plant. Short in-rack cross-links can use Cisco direct-attach cables where TMG lists them.[14]

06Uplink speeds

  • Edge → intermediate: 2 × 10G (Te1/1/1–2) = 20 Gbps per edge node, 1.2 Tbps across all 60.
  • Intermediate → CP/BN: 2 × 100G (Hu2/0/6–7) = 200 Gbps per intermediate, 800 Gbps in total.
  • CP/BN → fusion: 2 × 25G (Twe1/0/23 → FUS-1, Twe1/0/24 → FUS-2) = 50 Gbps per border and 100 Gbps in total, about 50 Mbps per user with all 2,000 active. 25G is an assumption, since the SFP28 ports also run at 1 or 10G to match the fusion device.

07Downlink speeds

  • CP/BN → intermediate: 4 × 100G (Hu1/0/25–28) = 400 Gbps per CP/BN.
  • Intermediate → edge: 30 × 10G = 300 Gbps per intermediate, out of 480 Gbps line-card capacity.
  • Edge → endpoints: 48 × 1G copper = 48 Gbps per edge node. UPOE delivers up to 60 W per port, within the PSU budget.

08TCAM architecture

  • UADP ASICs keep forwarding tables (FIB/LPM, host, adjacency, MAC) in hash-based memory and use TCAM for ACL, QoS and SGACL classification. The SDM template decides how both are partitioned.[1][2]
  • C9500-24Y4C: UADP 3.0 with a 416K double-width FIB (IPv4 and IPv6 at the same scale) and 54K ACL TCAM.[2]
  • C9400X-SUP-2XL: UADP 3.0sec. The custom template splits 416K FIB + 52K ACL, and line cards have no TCAM of their own.[8][6]
  • C9300-48U: UADP 2.0, where each IPv6 entry uses two entries.[10]

09TCAM limitations

  • Template changes need a reload, and every gain in one resource costs another. On the SUP-2XL a custom template isn't restored automatically after an RMA.[8]
  • FIB programming is hash-based, so real capacity depends on the prefix mix.[1]
  • IPv6 halves route capacity on the C9300 (UADP 2.0).[10]
  • SG-ACEs use security ACL TCAM on the enforcing edge. Catalyst Center lists 5,000 SG-ACEs for the 9300, and the data sheet lists 5,120 security ACL entries.[11][9]
  • Cisco doesn't publish per-resource values for the standard Access template on the SUP-2XL, so they're shown as “Depends on supervisor / line card / hardware configuration”.

10SDA scale considerations

  • The site has 62 fabric devices against a limit of 500–1,200 per site, depending on the Catalyst Center appliance. Intermediates don't count toward the limit.[11][12]
  • CP: 150,000 endpoints, with every IPv4 and IPv6 address counted, against about 6,000–8,000 for 2,000 users. Border: 48,000 IPv4 routes and 150,000 host entries.[11]
  • Per site: 64–256 L3 VNs and 100–1,000 IP pools, depending on the appliance. Each device supports 256 VNs.[11]
  • Two CPs of the same model are recommended. Use dedicated CPs if wireless roaming is frequent.[12]
  • Check the IOS XE and Catalyst Center releases against the SD-Access compatibility matrix.[13]

11Assumptions

  • About 2,000 users behind the edge nodes, with 3–4 endpoints each. Each C9500-24Y4C combines the CP, an anywhere border (CP + BN) and the underlay core joining both intermediate groups. Cisco calls co-location the most common deployment.[12]
  • C9500 model C9500-24Y4C. Each intermediate is a C9404R with a single SUP-2XL and one LC-48XS, with no intermediate cross-links. Edge nodes are standalone C9300-48U switches with an NM-8X.
  • Links are routed P2P /31 with IS-IS (LAN Automation or manual), BFD, PIM-SM and MTU 9100. The seed cross-link follows CVD guidance, including CLNS MTU 1400.[12]
  • The fusion or firewall pair (FUS-1, FUS-2) is out of scope. Each border has an assumed 25G VRF-lite handoff, with eBGP per VN, to each of them.
  • LISP Pub/Sub control plane: if a border loses its external routes, the edges switch to the other border, so the borders need no per-VN iBGP between them.[16]
  • Endpoint counts are estimates, and the optics shown are examples. Port positions in the diagram are schematic.
  • Software minimums: IOS XE 17.8+ for the LC-48XS, and 17.11.1+ for a custom SDM template on the SUP-2XL. Confirm licensing with Cisco.
